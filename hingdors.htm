Hingstons in Dorset

It is known that there were Hingstons in Dorset; it is assumed that the original ones were immigrants from Devon but the movement occurred fairly early. There seem to be two groups; possibly significantly, both groups are in towns with ports.
  • Family at Melcombe Regis. The earliest record is in the 1580s, so it is likely that the move from Devon occurred before we are likely to find any parish records. There is a possibility that this family is linked to the Irish Tree HN .
  • Family at Lyme Regis. We have records of a family at Lyme at least as early as the 1780s; they typically spelt the name as Hingeston. The historian HM#17 Francis Charles Hingeston and WEH both believed they were linked to the St Ives Hingstons in Tree HM and there is supposedly a document showing that connection, but it has not yet come to light. The information for this family is thus shown in Tree HM.

Melcombe family

Rosalind Dunning <ros@archbishops.demon.co.uk> dre my attention to this family. She is searching for an Elizabeth Hingston, born circa 1740, and has been looking in the Dorset records. She has found the following, which may link in with other people's studies:-

I had thought that my Elizabeth Hingston who married in Melcombe Regis, Dorset came from Devon. But I now think there had been Hingstons around at Melcombe for a while.

I've just obtained an Admon in respect of a Dorothy Hingston widow of a John Hingston of Weymouth dated 1685. The application to administer is made by a creditor, William Masterman of Fryer Wooden. 5 children renounce the administration. These are Mary, Dorothy, John, Samuel and Elizabeth Hingston. There is also an inventory of the late Dorothy's goods.
